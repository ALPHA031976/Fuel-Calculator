How the calculator works

The dial. The Federation chooses the value placed on its crude, from its production cost up to the world price or beyond. Petrol’s cost is that value plus the refinery toll and handling, shared equally across 159 litres, converted to naira, plus delivery and retail and any published levy.

The imported comparison. Imported petrol lands at the world crude price plus a premium for foreign refining, shipping and trading, converted to naira, plus the same delivery and retail cost.

The Federation’s receipts. Nigerians use about 44.4 million litres of petrol, 14.5 million of diesel and 3.0 million of jet fuel a day (NMDPRA, September 2026), about 389,000 barrels’ worth. For those barrels the Federation receives the value it places on the crude; consumers pay the toll and handling through the pump price. The rest of the Programme crude is refined and exported, assumed, cautiously, to earn no more than the world crude price less refining and handling. Receipts are compared with selling all the Programme crude at the world price. Upstream production costs are not deducted from either. All figures are illustrations.

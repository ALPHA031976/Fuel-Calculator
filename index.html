What stays true whatever you enter

No hemorrhaging. Every input is a cost or an openly chosen margin, so the price always covers what the fuel costs to make and deliver. Every routine operating cost has an identified source of revenue, and no fuel leaves the refinery unpaid.

Nothing hidden. If the price is set below what selling the crude would earn, the difference is shown above. That is the value returned to Nigerians as fairly priced fuel, and it belongs in the budget, approved and published.

The decision stays at home. World markets set import parity. Here, Nigerians choose the inputs and the margin.

Method: products follow a typical Nigerian refinery yield (52% petrol, 18% diesel, 19% jet fuel, 3% cooking gas, 5% naphtha), valued at early-October 2026 refining margins. Costs are shared across products by value. Home demand is 45 million litres of petrol a day plus other products; surplus is exported at world prices less $3 a barrel, with exporters keeping a quarter of the export margin plus $1 a barrel. Short-term finance is 10% a year for 20 days on the crude value. Import parity adds $5 a barrel for shipping and port costs. All figures are illustrative.
